Our Distance

Two years taught us that miles are just numbers.

Last month we closed our second year together.
Most couples count their anniversaries in dinners shared.
We count ours in time zones crossed, video calls that lasted until sunrise, and the quiet courage of loving someone who sleeps while you're awake.

I've stood on your streets in the Philippines once.
Felt the heat you always complain about.
Saw the jeepneys you described.
And now we're building the bridge for you to stand on mine, here in Chile, where the Andes meet the sky and I want you to see it all.

For my Wibe,

25 months ago I didn't know that a screen could hold so much love.
I didn't know that missing someone could become a kind of daily prayer, or that "good morning" and "good night" could exist in the same sentence when we're 17,000 kilometers apart.

I still remember landing in Manila.
The humidity hit me like a wall, but nothing hit me harder than seeing you walk toward me at the airport.
For a few days, the distance didn't exist.
We were just two people in one place, finally.
Eating at midnight, getting lost in the city, falling asleep without setting an alarm for a call.

Now we're saving.
Every peso, every skipped coffee, it all goes into the same dream.
You, here.
In Chile.
Where I can show you the Pacific from my window, where we can finally share a winter instead of swapping seasons, where I don't have to calculate what time it is in Manila before I text you "I love you."

They say long distance is hard.
They're right.
But they never tell you that it's also proof.
Proof that we choose this.
Every single day.
Proof that what we have is worth plane tickets, worth time zones, worth waiting for.

Month 25 isn't just a number.
It's 25 months of choosing each other despite the map between us.
And soon, Wibe, that map gets smaller.
Until it disappears completely.

Yours, always,
Daby
